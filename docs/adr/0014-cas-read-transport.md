# ADR-0014: CAS read transport and CLR lifetime

Accepted for the experimental engine, 13 September 2026. Replaces the Channel/Monitor read transport in ADR0005; its scheduling contract remains relevant.

Caffeine v3.2.4 `836b65c0a83e5d1641ded9c6de578654bc04b2e9` `BoundedBuffer`, `StripedBuffer`, `afterRead` and `skipReadBuffer` inform bounded CAS rings, lazy stripes, bounded retry and batch drain. This is source-informed adaptation, not clean-room work or a mechanical JVM memory-model translation.

FULL requests maintenance; bounded-retry FAILED may discard a policy observation. Reliable writes stay separate. Neither can weaken freshness, request statistics, entry/epoch fencing or value publication. Use CLR `Interlocked`/`Volatile`, not Java final-field, VarHandle, layout or opaque-access assumptions. An unpublished reserved head blocks only its stripe; never spin forever waiting for a producer.

Read scheduling revision, 30 September 2026:

- **R1 — Background backlog.** After a background pass, re-arm for reads only while a stripe has an immediately published head and at least a quarter of its capacity reserved. The threshold is fixed, with a minimum of one slot. A smaller readable tail remains delayable until a full offer, reliable write or explicit cleanup. This changes approximate recency/frequency replay, not lookup freshness or request accounting.
- **R2 — Requested drain.** Explicit `CleanUp` and the synchronous cleanup after an initially rejected read-maintenance request use `HasPublished`, without the background threshold. Set the drain request under the policy gate; it survives a running owner and is cleared under that same gate only when no head is immediately readable. Each pass still drains at most 256 reads/256 writes and each invocation at most 32 passes by default. Budget exhaustion, scheduler rejection or unpublished heads may leave work for a later attempt; no caller loops without a bound. The reliable-write fallback after budget exhaustion does not start another read-pass budget.
- **R3 — Full-stripe handoff.** Clear the read signal and re-check the selected condition before releasing the owner. A full stripe with a published head always meets the background threshold, so a racing full offer either claims the cleared signal or is covered by the re-check. Keep rejected-fallback retry recovery and reliable-write signalling independent.

The quarter-capacity threshold was selected over half capacity because their measured timing ranges overlap while a quarter delays fewer observations and stays closer to the previous scheduling contract. Both candidates passed the trace acceptance criteria. See the [dated comparison](../benchmarks/read-maintenance-20260930/README.md) for the measurements and limits.

Disposal closes admission and detaches/clears event storage without waiting for paused producers. Producer-held references may last until the call returns, but late publication must self-clear and cannot retain other payloads or revive the cache. Consumer/disposer exclusion, full-fence publication, sequence drop ownership and live-or-retired diagnostics aggregation are detailed in [concurrency](../concurrency.md).

A rejected re-arm after an accepted worker's budget exhausted originally left the read signal set. The regression `AcceptedWorkerThatCannotRearmAllowsTheNextFullHitToRetry` requires bounded reliable fallback to release the read signal under the policy gate, allowing a later FULL to retry. Scheduled/running owners still coalesce; accepted reads remain delayable until full, a write or explicit cleanup.

Cold-start bypass is deliberately narrower than Caffeine: built-in size policy, strong references, no expiry/refresh. Write maintenance initialises the sketch around half capacity; once enabled, shrinking residency does not restore bypass. Resize forces activation; clear starts a new policy generation. One release/acquire adapter flag avoids repeatedly reading policy/sketch objects on hits. Old observations still carry old exact tokens.

The production stripe capacity became 64 rather than 16 on 14 September to amortise .NET ThreadPool handoffs. Per-pass reads remain capped at 256. The metadata/root bound quadruples; active 64-bit ring payload grows by 768 bytes, allocated lazily. A 128-slot candidate regressed cycling reads and was rejected. The 64-slot choice had mixed individual measurements, including a .NET 8 single-reader cycling cost increase in one comparison; later same-binary A/A variation prevented attributing every timing difference to source. Native profiling placed the principal observed SpinWait caller in ThreadPool waiting, not an entry/policy lock; that is not a unique root-cause proof.

Acceptance compares equal configurations and reports accepted/drained/dropped events together with actual engine trace hit ratios; lower observation replay is not itself evidence of an equivalent-work speedup. Controlled saturation, reservation holes, wrap, capacity-one reuse, shutdown, handoff, multi-stripe progress and freshness regressions are required. The scheduling revision additionally covers below-threshold tails, explicit cleanup while an owner is running and rejected-scheduler cleanup across several passes. See [benchmark methodology](../benchmark-methodology.md) and [release readiness](../release-readiness.md) for measured scope; this design does not claim universal speedup or Caffeine parity.
